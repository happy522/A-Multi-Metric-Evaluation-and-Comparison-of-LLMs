Changes to How the Antibiotic Azithromycin Is Used
Goal: Use the medicine better and stop germs from becoming resistant.

European health experts have suggested changes to how the antibiotic azithromycin is used in Europe. Some uses will be removed. These suggestions aim to use this common antibiotic better. They also want to stop antimicrobial resistance. This happens when germs become resistant to medicines, and the medicines stop working.

Azithromycin has been used for many years to treat many infections in children and adults. The World Health Organization (WHO) lists it as an essential medicine. This means it is very important for public health.

However, the WHO also says this antibiotic has a higher risk of causing resistance. It is on the WHO Watch list. Data shows that resistance to this antibiotic has grown in recent years.

Medicines on the Watch list should be used carefully and monitored. However, data shows that use of azithromycin has increased recently. A study showed this antibiotic is used widely across Europe by both adults and children.

To promote wise use and keep the medicine effective, the experts looked at the benefits and risks again. They looked at azithromycin taken by mouth or given through a vein.

The experts reviewed all available data. This included study results, information about germs, risk assessments, and current treatment rules.

Changes to Approved Uses

Based on this review, the experts recommended changing most approved uses of azithromycin. The changes match the uses with the latest data and make them more clear. They also aim to make dosing, warnings, and information the same for all products. This includes information on mixing with other medicines, use in pregnancy, side effects, and study data.

The changes mainly affect:

* Infections of the nose, throat, airways, and lungs. Examples include sinusitis, tonsillitis, bronchitis, and pneumonia.
* Sexually transmitted diseases. Examples include infections caused by Chlamydia or Gonorrhoea.
* Infections of the female reproductive system. An example is pelvic inflammatory disease.
* Dental infections. Examples include abscesses and gum disease.
* Treatment and prevention of certain infections in people living with HIV.

The full list of changed uses is in the published medicine information.
