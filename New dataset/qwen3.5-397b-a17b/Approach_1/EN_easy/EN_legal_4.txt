The Commission proposes a 5-point plan to make EU external borders stronger and safer

Today, the Commission is suggesting a 5-point plan. It aims to stop, predict, and handle sudden groups of people entering illegally. This will make the EU's outer borders more secure.

The EU already has a complete set of rules to manage migration. These rules have worked well. Illegal crossings are at their lowest level since 2021. But recent events have tested these borders. This happened from the Eastern borders to the Mediterranean Sea. The crisis in Ceuta this summer showed how quickly large groups can arrive and cause emergencies.

President von der Leyen said in her speech that summer events show we need better tools. This is especially true for emergencies.

These situations put our safety and unity at risk. They also cause people to lose their lives. Protecting EU borders is a job for all European countries. We need to work together and help each other.

Because of Ceuta, President von der Leyen listed five key areas for action. Based on the 2026 Migration Strategy, the Commission explains what to do in these areas. They will use old tools and make new ones if needed.

1. Working more with partner countries to stop illegal migration

Working closely with other countries on watching borders and sharing information is important. Key areas include:

- Fix the reasons why people leave their homes. Create jobs and opportunities in their home countries. There is a new plan for youth skills and jobs in the Mediterranean. There will also be new offices to help people move to the EU legally and safely.
- Manage crises better with partner countries. Use systems to warn early about problems.
- EU agencies (Frontex, Europol, Eurojust) will work closer with partner countries. They will share information about risks and migration routes.
- Make new coordination centers with partner countries. This will help share information about risks before people reach the border.

2. Making EU external borders stronger

Each country is responsible for its own borders, but the EU will help more during emergencies. This includes:

- Create a new plan for emergencies. This protects borders at all times. It helps when large groups are used as weapons against the EU, as President von der Leyen said.
- Change the rules for Frontex (the EU border agency). They will have more staff and equipment ready to go quickly. They will help more with sending people back. They will work closer with other countries and watch the borders better.
- Give specific help to countries to strengthen weak parts of the border.
- Give help through the EU Civil Protection system if countries ask for it.
