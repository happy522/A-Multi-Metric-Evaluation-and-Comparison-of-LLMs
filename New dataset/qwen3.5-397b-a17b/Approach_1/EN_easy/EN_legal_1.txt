Consumer Groups Take Stronger Action to Protect Gamers' Rights

Today, the Consumer Protection Cooperation (CPC) Network started working together. This is a group of agencies from the European Union (EU). They are taking action against nine video game companies. Their goal is to protect the rights of people who play games.

These actions follow important rules about money used inside games. These rules make sure companies are clear and fair when players buy virtual money.

The agencies want to make sure companies follow these rules:

*   Show clear prices: Show the real money cost of items and virtual money needed to play.
*   Do not hide costs: Do not use too many different types of money that make the real price hard to understand.
*   Do not force purchases: Players should not feel forced to buy too much virtual money to enjoy the game or move forward.
*   Give clear information before buying: Players must understand the details before they buy virtual money or items.
*   Respect the right to cancel: Players should know they can cancel a purchase within 14 days, even for virtual money they have not used.
*   Use fair rules: The game's rules and conditions should be fair and easy to read.
*   Protect vulnerable players: Companies must protect all players, including children and others who might need extra help.

Today, the group also agreed on a shared plan. This plan protects gamers' rights across all EU countries.

Background Information

The CPC Network is made of official groups from different countries. They make sure EU consumer laws are followed. They work together with the European Commission. They stop companies from breaking laws that hurt people in more than one country.

In 2025, the Network talked to video game companies to follow the rules. The talks did not work well. So, the Network checked the market and found several games that need more checking. These games include Hunt: Showdown 1896, Forge of Empires, Candy Crush Saga, Minecraft, Mech Arena, Gardenscapes, Valorant, Clash of Clans, and For Honor. The investigations today are about the companies that make these games, such as Crytek, InnoGames, King.com, Mojang, Plarium, PLR Worldwide, Riot Games, Supercell, and Ubisoft.

At the same time, the Network is working with Activision Blizzard. They want to make sure the games Diablo Immortal and Call of Duty Mobile follow EU laws. They are checking how virtual money is sold. They are also checking how player data is collected. They want to know if games are designed to be addictive. They are checking parental controls, marketing to children, information shown before buying, and how game accounts are blocked.
