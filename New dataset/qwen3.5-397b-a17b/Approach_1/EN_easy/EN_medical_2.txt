EMA Says Levamisole Medicines Should Be Removed from the Market
Serious Brain Damage Confirmed as a Side Effect of Levamisole

The EMA safety committee recommends stopping the sale of medicines containing levamisole in the EU. They reviewed the medicine carefully. They found that the risks are now higher than the benefits for treating worm infections in adults and children.

The review confirmed that levamisole can cause a rare but serious side effect called leukoencephalopathy. This condition damages the white matter of the brain. The white matter has nerve fibers covered by a protective layer. This layer helps parts of the brain talk to each other. This damage can be very serious and life-threatening. It is also hard to diagnose.

Brain damage can happen after just one dose of levamisole. Symptoms might appear up to several months after treatment. There is no way to prevent this risk. No group of people is safer than others. Other medicines for worm infections exist in the EU. Levamisole treats mild infections. But the brain damage is serious and unpredictable. So, the committee says the risks are too high. They recommend removing these medicines from the EU market.

This decision is based on new safety data. People reported serious brain and nerve damage after using levamisole. The committee also read scientific studies. They listened to independent experts and the World Health Organization.

The EMA always checks medicine safety in the EU. If risks are higher than benefits, they act to protect public health. Removing levamisole shows their commitment to safe and effective medicines.

Information for Patients

- The EMA recommends removing levamisole medicines from the EU market. These medicines treat parasitic worm infections.
- The safety committee confirmed that levamisole can cause serious brain damage.
- Other medicines for worm infections are available in the EU.
- If you took levamisole, see a doctor right away if you have weak muscles, trouble speaking, confusion, or trouble controlling movements.
- These symptoms can happen after one dose. They might start up to several months after treatment.
- Talk to your doctor if you have questions about this medicine for you or your child.
