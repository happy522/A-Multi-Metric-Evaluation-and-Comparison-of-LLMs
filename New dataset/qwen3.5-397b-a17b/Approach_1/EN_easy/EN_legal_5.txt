Commission Takes Bulgaria to Court to Ensure EU Electricity Rules Are Followed

Today, the European Commission decided to take Bulgaria to the EU Court of Justice. This is because Bulgaria did not change its own laws to match EU rules about the electricity market.

The EU law sets important rules for how the electricity sector works. The goal is to make electricity markets across the EU work together, be fair, and focus on consumers. It also wants to protect consumers and let them take part in the energy market. A working electricity market is needed to give people and businesses safe, cheap, and clean energy.

Bulgaria needed to adopt these rules by December 31, 2020. The Commission sent warning letters in 2021 and 2023 because Bulgaria did not change all the laws. After checking Bulgaria's answers, the Commission believes Bulgaria still has not fully adopted the rules. There are still problems with access to the power network, rules for building new power plants, and consumers switching energy providers. There are also issues with the company that manages power transmission, especially regarding digital tools and data. Additionally, there are problems with the national energy authority's powers and duties.

This decision is part of the Commission's work to fix problems in the EU Single Market. Because of this, the Commission is asking the Court to make Bulgaria pay a fine.

The Commission is taking legal action to make sure the EU electricity market works well and to protect consumers. The Commission must make sure EU laws are correctly made into national laws. Currently, the Commission is taking legal action against 10 EU countries because they did not fully adopt these electricity rules by the 2020 deadline.
