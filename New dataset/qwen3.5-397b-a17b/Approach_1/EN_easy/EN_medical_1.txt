EMA Recommends Stopping Sale of Tavneos
Risks Are Greater Than Benefits

The European Medicines Agency (EMA) committee has finished checking Tavneos. They say the medicine should not be sold in the European Union anymore. This is because the risks are now greater than the benefits. Tavneos is used for adults with serious blood vessel diseases called GPA or MPA.

The check started because of new information. This information showed problems with the main study used to approve the medicine. The study had 331 patients. It compared Tavneos to a dummy treatment. At first, the study showed Tavneos worked as well as or better than steroid medicines to help patients get better.

But after looking at all the data, the committee found the study did not follow proper rules. The data given earlier was wrong and misleading. It cannot be trusted to show Tavneos works. Other data is not enough to prove the benefits.

So, the committee says stop selling the medicine in the EU. They listened to patients and doctors too. If the European Commission agrees, Tavneos will not be allowed in the EU anymore.

New patients should not start taking Tavneos. Patients already taking it should change to other medicines.

Tavneos can cause serious liver damage. This includes a rare problem where small tubes in the liver get damaged and disappear. Some cases have led to death. Patients must have their liver checked closely until they stop taking the medicine completely.

For patients who took Tavneos for less than three months, check liver function every two weeks until three months have passed since starting. For patients who took it for longer than three months, check every four weeks for up to six months. After that, check when needed. If there are signs of serious liver damage, stop Tavneos right away.
