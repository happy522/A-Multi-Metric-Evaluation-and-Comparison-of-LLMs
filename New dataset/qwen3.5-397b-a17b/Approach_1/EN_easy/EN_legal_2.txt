**European Commission Fines Kingspan €40 Million for Giving Wrong Information**

The European Commission has fined Kingspan €40 million. This is because Kingspan gave incorrect and misleading information during an investigation in 2021. The investigation was about Kingspan's plan to buy another company called Trimo. Kingspan eventually cancelled the purchase.

Both companies make special panels used for building and insulating industrial and commercial buildings.

**The Investigation**
In November 2022, the Commission started an investigation. They wanted to know if Kingspan gave wrong information on purpose or by carelessness.

In March 2024, the Commission told Kingspan it broke the rules in six ways. Kingspan replied to these claims. After looking at Kingspan's answers, the Commission removed two of the claims. They decided Kingspan broke the rules in four specific ways:

*   Kingspan gave misleading information about how they track how much their products are used.
*   Kingspan gave wrong information about records of bids they won and lost.
*   Kingspan gave wrong information about what their board members knew regarding the purchase and business strategy.
*   Kingspan gave wrong information about their research and development work.

Kingspan provided this wrong information in official forms or when answering questions from the Commission.

**The Fine**
Under EU rules, the Commission can fine companies up to 1% of their total sales if they give wrong information. The fine depends on how serious the mistake was. It must be high enough to stop companies from doing it again.

The Commission says Kingspan's mistakes were serious. They made the investigation difficult. Kingspan should have known the rules. In some cases, Kingspan claimed certain documents did not exist or that they could not provide information, but this was not true.

Because of this, the Commission fined Kingspan €10 million for each of the four mistakes. The total fine is €40 million. The Commission believes this amount is fair and will prevent future rule breaks.
