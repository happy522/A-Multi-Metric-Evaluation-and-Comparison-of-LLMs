Changes to the use of antibiotic azithromycin

We want to use azithromycin better and stop it making bad bacteria stronger.

The European Medicine Agency (EMA) has made new rules for using azithromycin in the European Union. These rules are to help use azithromycin correctly and stop bad bacteria becoming stronger.

Azithromycin is a common antibiotic that has been used for many years to treat many different infections in children and adults. It is very important for public health.

However, azithromycin can make bad bacteria stronger. This is a problem because it means the antibiotic does not work as well. The World Health Organization (WHO) has put azithromycin in a list of medicines that need to be used carefully.

We have used azithromycin a lot in recent years. A study showed that it is used a lot in the European Union, both in adults and children.

To use azithromycin correctly, the EMA has looked at all the information about the benefits and risks of this antibiotic. They have reviewed data from clinical studies and information about resistance of bad bacteria.

Changes to the use of azithromycin

The EMA has made new rules for using azithromycin. These rules are to make sure we use azithromycin correctly and stop bad bacteria becoming stronger.

The new rules mainly concern:

- Infections of the nose, throat, airways and lungs, such as sinusitis and pneumonia;
- Sexually transmitted diseases, such as infections caused by Chlamydia and gonorrhoea;
- Infections of the female reproductive system, such as pelvic inflammatory disease;
- Dental infections, such as abscesses and periodontitis;
- Infections caused by Mycobacterium avium complex in people with HIV-1 infection.

The full list of revised uses can be found in the published product information.
