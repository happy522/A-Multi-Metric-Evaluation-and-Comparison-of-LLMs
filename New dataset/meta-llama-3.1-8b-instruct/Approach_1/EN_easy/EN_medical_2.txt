EMA says stop selling medicines with levamisole
Levamisole can cause serious brain damage
EMA's safety team says medicines with levamisole should not be sold in the EU. This is because the good these medicines do is not as good as the bad things they can cause. 

The review found that levamisole can cause a rare but serious brain problem. This problem damages the white part of the brain, which is made of nerve fibres covered by a protective layer. This can make people very sick and even life-threatening. 

The review found that this brain problem can happen after one dose of levamisole and can take months to appear. There is no way to stop this problem from happening. 

There are other medicines in the EU that can treat the same problem as levamisole. Since levamisole is used for mild problems and can cause serious brain damage, EMA says it is not safe to sell these medicines in the EU.

EMA checks the safety of medicines all the time. If a medicine is not safe, EMA will stop it from being sold. This is to protect people's health. 

Information for patients:

- EMA says stop selling medicines with levamisole in the EU.
- A review found that medicines with levamisole can cause serious brain damage.
- There are other medicines in the EU that can treat the same problem as levamisole.
- If you have been treated with a medicine with levamisole and have muscle weakness, difficulty speaking, confusion or difficulty controlling movements, see a doctor right away.
- These symptoms can happen after one dose of levamisole and can take months to appear.
- If you have questions about your or your child's past or present treatment with a medicine with levamisole, ask your doctor.
