Commission decides to refer Bulgaria to the Court of Justice of the European Union to ensure full transposition of EU electricity market rules
Today, the European Commission decided to refer Bulgaria (INFR(2021)0015) to the Court of Justice of the European Union for failing to transpose into national legislation Directive (EU) 2019/944 on common rules for the internal market for electricity, amending Directive 2012/27/EU. 
Directive (EU) 2019/944 lays down key rules on the organisation and functioning of the EU electricity sector with a view to creating integrated, competitive, consumer-centred, flexible, fair and transparent electricity markets across the EU. It also aims to ensure high levels of consumer protection and to enable all consumers to participate actively in the energy market. A fully functioning internal electricity market is essential to deliver secure, affordable and clean energy to citizens and businesses in the Union.
Bulgaria was required to transpose the Directive by 31 December 2020. The Commission sent Bulgaria a letter of formal notice in February 2021 and a reasoned opinion in April 2023 after concluding that not all provisions of the Directive had been transposed into national law. Having examined the replies submitted by Bulgaria and the notified national transposition measures, the Commission considers that Bulgaria has still not fully transposed the Directive. In particular, the Commission considers that key gaps remain as regards third-party access to the network, the authorisation procedure for new generation capacity, consumers' right to participate in collective switching schemes, and requirements linked to the use of flexibility in distribution networks. The Commission also found shortcomings concerning the tasks of the transmission system operator, notably as regards digitalisation, data management and the provision of non-frequency ancillary services, as well as the powers and duties of the national regulatory authority, including the monitoring of generation and storage investment, self-generated electricity and citizen energy communities, and the approval of methodologies for ancillary services.
The decision is part of the Commission's enforcement efforts to remove barriers in the Single Market in 11 focus areas, as announced in the Communication “A simpler, clearer and better enforced EU Rulebook” (COM(2026) 380 final, Annex II). Therefore, the Commission is referring Bulgaria to the Court of Justice of the European Union and requesting the Court to impose financial sanctions in accordance with Article 260(3) TFEU.
The Commission is taking legal steps to ensure the proper functioning of the internal electricity market across the EU and to protect consumers. As guardian of the Treaties, the Commission has to ensure the full and correct transposition of EU law into national legislation. When it comes to the enforcement of Directive (EU) 2019/944, the Commission currently has infringement procedures open against 10 Member States for failing to notify the complete transposition measures of the Directive by the deadline of 31 December 2020.
