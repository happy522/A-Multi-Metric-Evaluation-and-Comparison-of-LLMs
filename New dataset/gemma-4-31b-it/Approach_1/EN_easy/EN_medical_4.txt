**Changes to how the antibiotic azithromycin is used**

**The goal is to use the medicine better and stop bacteria from becoming too strong**

The EMA (European Medicines Agency) wants to change how the antibiotic azithromycin is used in the EU. They want to stop using it for some things. This is to make sure the medicine works well and to stop "antimicrobial resistance." This is when germs change so that medicines cannot kill them anymore.

Azithromycin has been used for a long time to treat many infections in children and adults. The World Health Organization (WHO) says it is a very important medicine.

However, the WHO also says that azithromycin has a high risk of making bacteria resistant. Data shows that more bacteria are becoming resistant to this medicine.

Because of this, doctors should be very careful when using it. But data shows that people are using azithromycin more than before. A study showed it is used a lot across the EU.

The EMA looked at the benefits and risks of using this medicine (taken by mouth or by a drip in the vein). They looked at clinical studies, how bacteria react, and current medical guidelines.

**Changes to how the medicine is used**

The EMA recommends changing the rules for most uses of azithromycin. They want the rules to match the newest data. They also want the instructions for dose, side effects, and use during pregnancy to be the same for all brands of the medicine.

The changes mostly affect these types of infections:

*   **Breathing infections:** Infections of the nose, throat, and lungs (like sinusitis or pneumonia).
*   **STIs:** Infections passed through sex (like chlamydia or gonorrhea).
*   **Female reproductive system:** Infections in the pelvic area.
*   **Dental infections:** Infections in the gums or teeth.
*   **HIV-1:** Treating and preventing certain infections in people with HIV-1.

You can find the full list of changes in the product information.
