Changes to the use of antibiotic azithromycin  
The medicine committee of EMA has made new rules for the antibiotic azithromycin.  
These rules are to make sure the medicine is used in the best way.  
They also want to stop germs from becoming resistant to the medicine.  
Resistance means the medicine stops working against germs.  

Azithromycin has been used for many years to treat different infections.  
It is used for children and adults.  
It is on a list of important medicines from the World Health Organization (WHO).  
This means it is very important for public health.  

But WHO also says azithromycin is a medicine that has a higher risk of resistance.  
It is in a group called Watch.  
Data show that resistance to this medicine has gone up in recent years.  

Medicines in the Watch group need to be used carefully.  
But data show that use of azithromycin has gone up.  
A study from EMA showed that this medicine is used a lot across the EU.  
It is used for adults and children.  

To make sure the medicine is used in a better way, the committee looked at the benefits and risks.  
They looked at the medicine when it is given by mouth or by drip into a vein.  
They checked all the different uses.  

The committee looked at all the information.  
This includes results from studies, information about resistance, and treatment guidelines.  

The committee decided to change most of the uses for azithromycin.  
The changes are to make the uses match the latest information.  
They also want to make the dosing and warnings the same for all products.  
They also want to make information about side effects and pregnancy the same.  

The changes are mainly about:  
- Infections in the nose, throat, airways, and lungs, like sinusitis, tonsillitis, bronchitis, and pneumonia.  
- Sexually transmitted diseases, like chlamydia and gonorrhoea.  
- Infections in the female reproductive system, like pelvic inflammatory disease.  
- Dental infections, like abscesses and gum disease.  
- Treatment and prevention of a type of infection in people with HIV.  

The full list of changes is in the product information.
