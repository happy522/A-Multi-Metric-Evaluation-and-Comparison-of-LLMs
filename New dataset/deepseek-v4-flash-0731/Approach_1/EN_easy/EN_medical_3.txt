Ixchiq: The temporary stop on giving the vaccine to people 65 years and older will be removed.

The vaccine will only be used when there is a big risk of chikungunya disease, and after doctors think carefully about the good things and the bad things.

EMA’s safety committee (called PRAC) has finished checking Ixchiq (a vaccine for chikungunya that uses a weakened virus). This was because some people had serious side effects.

The temporary stop on giving the vaccine to people aged 65 years and older, which was in place while the check was happening, will now be removed.

But the committee said that, for people of all ages, the vaccine should only be given when there is a big risk of getting chikungunya, and after doctors think carefully about the good things and the bad things.

What the safety information shows

Serious side effects from the vaccine happened mostly in people 65 years and older, and in people who already had other health problems. These side effects made their health problems worse, or made them feel more unwell overall. In some cases, this led to going to hospital or death.

Many of the serious side effects are like the symptoms of chikungunya disease. These include fever, feeling unwell, not wanting to eat, and confusion. Confusion can make people fall. These chikungunya-like symptoms are usually mild, but some adults (about 2 out of 100 people) may get more serious symptoms.

The committee also looked at cases of encephalitis (swelling in the brain). Symptoms include confusion, sleepiness, fever, and headache. Cases of encephalitis are rare, and we do not know how often they happen.

The good things and bad things for older people

Most serious side effects happened in older people. But Ixchiq works well at helping the body make antibodies against the chikungunya virus. This may be especially helpful for older people, because they have a higher risk of getting very sick from chikungunya.

So the committee said the vaccine should only be given when there is a big risk of getting chikungunya, and after doctors think carefully about the good things and the bad things.

People with weak immune systems

Doctors are reminded that Ixchiq must not be given to people whose immune system is weak because of disease or medical treatment. People with weak immune systems have a higher risk of problems from vaccines that use weakened live viruses, like Ixchiq.

The vaccine was already not allowed for people with weak immune systems, and this rule stays the same. The information about Ixchiq will be updated with the new advice after this check.

Ixchiq was approved in the EU in June 2024. When the check started, about 36,000 doses of the vaccine had been used around the world.

Information for the public

•	Some people have had serious side effects after getting the Ixchiq vaccine. These people were mostly 65 years and older, or people with long-term health problems.
•	Most serious side effects are like the symptoms of chikungunya disease. They include fever, feeling unwell, not wanting to eat, and confusion. Confusion can make people fall.
•	There have also been rare cases of encephalitis (swelling in the brain). Symptoms include confusion, sleepiness, fever, and headache. If you have these symptoms, get medical help right away.
•	Tell your doctor if your general health or another health problem gets worse soon after getting the vaccine.
•	Ixchiq works well at helping the body make antibodies against the chikungunya virus. This may be especially helpful for older people, because they have a higher risk of getting very sick from chikungunya.
•	Before you get Ixchiq, your doctor will think about your risk of getting chikungunya, and will carefully weigh the good things and the bad things for you if you take the vaccine.
•	If you have any questions about getting the vaccine, talk to your doctor.
