EMA says: Stop selling medicines with levamisole in the EU.
Levamisole can hurt the brain. This is a serious problem.
EMA’s safety group looked at these medicines. They found that levamisole is not safe enough to use for worm infections. The good effects do not beat the bad effects anymore.
The safety group found that levamisole can cause a brain disease called leukoencephalopathy. This disease hurts the white part of the brain. The white part helps the brain talk to itself. This disease can make people very sick. It can even kill them. It is hard for doctors to find this disease.
The safety group saw that this brain disease can happen after just one dose of levamisole. It can show up many months after taking the medicine. The group could not find a way to stop this risk. They could not find who is more likely to get it. There are other medicines for worm infections in the EU. So, the safety group said: stop selling levamisole medicines in the EU.
The safety group made this choice after looking at new information. They saw reports of people getting brain damage from levamisole. They also read science papers. They talked to brain doctors and infection experts. They also talked to the World Health Organization.
EMA always checks the safety of medicines in the EU. If a medicine becomes too risky, EMA acts to keep people safe. Stopping levamisole medicines shows EMA wants all medicines in the EU to be safe, work well, and be good quality.
Information for patients
•	EMA said to stop selling levamisole medicines in the EU. Some EU countries used these medicines for worm infections.
•	EMA’s safety group found that levamisole can cause leukoencephalopathy. This is a serious side effect that hurts the brain.
•	There are other medicines for worm infections in the EU.
•	If you took levamisole and feel weak in your muscles, have trouble talking, feel confused, or cannot control your movements, see a doctor right away.
•	These signs can happen after one dose of levamisole. They can also show up many months after you stop taking it.
•	If you have questions about your or your child’s treatment with levamisole, talk to your doctor.
