The Commission has a plan with 5 parts. The plan is to make the outside borders of the EU stronger and safer. It will help stop big groups of people coming in without permission, and it will help the EU be ready if this happens suddenly.

The EU already has rules to manage people coming in. These rules work well. Fewer people are coming in without permission now than in 2021. But this summer, something happened in a place called Ceuta. Many people came all at once, and it was very hard to handle. This shows the EU needs better tools for emergencies.

The leader of the Commission, President von der Leyen, said we need to change our tools. This is important for emergencies. When many people come at once, it can be dangerous. People can die. It also makes the EU less safe and less united. Keeping the outside borders safe is a job for all EU countries together. We must work as one team and help each other.

President von der Leyen made a plan with 5 main parts. This plan builds on an older plan from January 2026. The Commission now says what to do in each part. Different groups will do different jobs. Some tools we already have will be made better. Some new tools will be made.

Part 1: Work with other countries to stop people coming without permission
We will work more with countries outside the EU. This includes watching for problems, giving early warnings, and sharing information. This helps stop illegal migration. Important things we will do:
- Help fix the reasons why people leave their home countries. We will create new chances for young people in places like the Mediterranean area. We will also open new Gateway Offices to help people come to the EU in safe and legal ways.
- Make sure our partnerships with other countries have strong plans for emergencies. This includes early warning systems and clear rules.
- Have Frontex, Europol, and Eurojust work more closely with partner countries. They will share information, study risks and dangers, and learn about migration paths.
- Create EUROSUR Coordination Centres with partner countries. These centres will help share information and check risks before people reach the border.

Part 2: Make the EU outside borders stronger
Each EU country is mainly responsible for its own borders. But the EU will help more to stop and handle future emergencies. This includes:
- Making a new European Emergency Response Framework. This will keep EU borders safe at all times, especially if big groups of people are sent on purpose to cause problems. President von der Leyen said this in her speech.
- The Commission will suggest giving Frontex more power. Frontex will have a bigger team of border guards ready to go quickly. It will also have a bigger role in sending people back, work more with partner countries, and watch the borders and nearby areas better.
- Give special help to EU countries to make weak parts of the outside borders stronger.
- If an EU country asks for help, the EU can send support through the Union Civil Protection Mechanism.
