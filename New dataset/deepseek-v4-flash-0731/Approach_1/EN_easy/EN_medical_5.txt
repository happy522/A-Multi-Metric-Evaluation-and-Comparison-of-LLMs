Benefits of sodium oxybate still bigger than risks for people with alcohol problems
More studies needed to learn more about how well it works
EMA’s medicines committee (CHMP) has said that sodium oxybate syrup and oral solution (175 mg/ml) still has more benefits than risks when used to treat alcohol withdrawal and to help people with alcohol problems stay away from alcohol for a long time. But there are some gaps and unclear things in the information we have, so more studies are needed to learn more about how well these medicines work for these uses.
The review started because the French medicines agency asked about how well sodium oxybate works for alcohol problems, based on information from three studies. There were also worries about the risk of people using the medicine in a wrong or harmful way, because it can affect the brain, and whether the current ways to stop this are good enough.
During the review, the CHMP looked at all the information, including from clinical studies, science papers, and reports from after the medicine was sold. The review found that the studies done after the medicines were first approved had some problems in how they were done, which made it unclear how well the treatment works. The Committee also said that science and study methods have gotten better since the first studies were done. So, the CHMP has said that the companies must do new studies to learn more about how well the medicines work. If the European Commission agrees, the companies must finish these studies and send the reports to national authorities, or the medicines might not stay approved.
For safety, the medicines’ safety is still the same as what we already know. The review did not find any new safety worries about the risk of wrong or harmful use of sodium oxybate for these uses, so there was no need to change the extra safety steps already in place.
The Committee said that the balance of benefits and risks for sodium oxybate medicines for these uses is still good, as long as the companies do the needed studies to get more information about how well the medicines work.
The CHMP opinion will now go to the European Commission, which will make a final decision that all EU countries must follow.
Information for patients
•	In Austria and Italy, medicines with sodium oxybate are used alone or with other medicines, to treat acute alcohol withdrawal in adults who have used alcohol for a long time. In Austria, they are also used with other medicines to help people with alcohol problems stay away from alcohol for a long time.
•	The medicines will still be available in Italy and Austria for these uses, but new studies will be done to get more information about how well they work.
•	There are no new worries about the safety of these medicines.
•	If you use a medicine with sodium oxybate for acute alcohol withdrawal or to help stay away from alcohol for a long time, and you have questions about your treatment, talk to your doctor or pharmacist.
