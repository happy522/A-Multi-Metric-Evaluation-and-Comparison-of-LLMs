Consumer protection bosses are doing more to keep gamers safe.

Today, a group of people who watch over consumer rights in Europe started checking nine video game companies. They want to make sure gamers are treated fairly.

This group is called the CPC Network. They made some rules to help gamers. These rules are about money and things you buy inside games.

The group wants game companies to do these things:

- Show the real money price for things you buy in the game. Do not hide the cost.
- Do not use tricky money systems that make it hard to know what you pay.
- Do not make gamers buy too much game money to play or have fun.
- Give clear and easy information before you buy anything in the game.
- Tell gamers they can change their mind and get their money back within 14 days, even for game money they have not used.
- Make the game rules fair and easy to understand.
- Keep all people safe, especially kids and people who need extra help.

The CPC Network also made a shared plan to protect gamers in all of Europe.

About the CPC Network
The CPC Network is a team of people from different countries in Europe. They work together to make sure companies follow the law. The European Commission helps them.

In 2025, they talked to the video game industry about these rules. The industry did not fix the problems, so the CPC Network checked many games. They found some games that need more checking. These games are: Hunt: Showdown 1896, Forge of Empires, Candy Crush Saga, Minecraft, Mech Arena, Gardenscapes, Valorant, Clash of Clans, and For Honor.

The companies they are checking are Crytek GmbH, InnoGames GmbH, King.com Operations (Malta) Limited, Mojang AB, Plarium Europe S.à.r.l., PLR Worldwide Sales Limited, Riot Games Limited, Supercell Oy, and Ubisoft EMEA SAS.

They are also checking a company called Activision Blizzard UK Limited. They want to make sure the games Diablo Immortal and Call of Duty Mobile follow the law. They are looking at how game money is sold, how player information is collected, if games are too addictive, how parent controls work, if ads are shown to kids, what information is given before you buy, and if game accounts are blocked unfairly.
