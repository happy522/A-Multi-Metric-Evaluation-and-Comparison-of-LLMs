Commission registers European Citizens' Initiative for sovereign European AI domains

Today, the European Commission registered the European Citizens' Initiative (ECI) called 'Creation of a European Digital Space (.IA) for Sovereign AI Governance'.

The organisers ask the Commission "to suggest a plan for high-trust digital spaces and to act at European level to talk with ISO and ICANN to keep the .IA domain safe". The initiative has three goals: to "suggest an EU plan that supports 'High-Trust Digital Spaces' and test areas (sandboxes) for ethical AI in the EU"; to "talk with ISO … and ICANN/IANA to reserve, protect, and give out the .IA name as part of European digital control"; and to "make sure that signing up and using the .IA domain includes Safe Digital Identity (eIDAS 2.0), clear AI checks (AI Act), and protection of personal data (GDPR)".

Because this initiative meets the formal rules in the law, the Commission says it is allowed under the European Citizens' Initiative Regulation. The Commission has not looked at the ideas in detail yet. Registering it does not mean the Commission will agree with it or take any action. The Commission will only decide on the initiative if it meets the rules, like getting at least one million valid signatures from EU citizens.

Next steps

After today's registration, the organisers have six months to start the 12-month period for collecting signatures. If the ECI gets at least one million valid signatures, with minimum numbers in at least seven EU countries, the Commission must respond and decide what, if anything, to do, and explain its choice.

Background

The ECI was created with the Lisbon Treaty as a way for citizens to set the agenda. It started in April 2012. Once registered, a European Citizens' Initiative lets one million citizens from at least seven EU countries ask the European Commission to suggest new laws in areas where it can act. The rules for acceptance are: (1) the action is not clearly outside the Commission's power to suggest laws, (2) it is not clearly abusive, silly, or annoying, and (3) it is not clearly against the values of the EU as in Article 2 of the EU Treaty and the rights in the EU Charter of Fundamental Rights.

Since the start of the European Citizens' Initiative, the Commission has registered 136 initiatives.

The content of the initiative only shows the views of the organisers and does not reflect the views of the Commission.
