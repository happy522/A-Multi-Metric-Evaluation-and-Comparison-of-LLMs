The Commission has decided to send Bulgaria to the Court of Justice of the European Union. This is because Bulgaria did not put all the new EU rules about electricity into its own laws.

These rules are about how electricity markets work in Europe. They help make sure that electricity markets are fair, open, and good for people. They also help protect people who use electricity and let them take part in the energy market.

Bulgaria had to put these rules into its laws by 31 December 2020. The Commission told Bulgaria about this problem in February 2021 and again in April 2023. But Bulgaria still has not put all the rules into its laws.

Some important parts are still missing. For example, rules about who can use the electricity network, how new power plants are approved, and how people can work together to switch electricity providers. There are also problems with how the electricity system operator does its job, like using digital tools and managing data. And there are issues with the national authority that checks the electricity market.

The Commission is doing this to remove problems in the Single Market. This is part of a bigger plan to make EU rules simpler and better enforced. The Commission is asking the Court to make Bulgaria pay money as a penalty.

The Commission wants to make sure the electricity market works well for everyone in the EU and protects people who use electricity. Bulgaria is one of 10 countries that did not finish putting these rules into their laws on time.
